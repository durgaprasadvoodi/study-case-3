The bet, in one sentence

Vault stops being a folder you visit and becomes a standing watch on your property — everything important lives in one place, safe is the default state, and your attention is only pulled in when something actually needs it.

The problem, for whom

A filing cabinet can't answer the one question that matters.

The person Vault is built for isn't managing one deal — they're carrying a portfolio accumulated over a lifetime: a flat they bought, a place they inherited, family land in their hometown, watched from a distance.

They earn well, have no time to sit in a sub-registrar's office, and aren't a power user of software. If something takes more than two taps to understand, they'll call someone instead.

"I am on this screen to ___"

Home

Check whether anything about my properties needs my attention today — without opening a single document.

Every other screen exists to either answer that question in more detail, or to keep the answer accurate.

Documents tab

Find one specific document I have somewhere — a photo on my phone, a PDF in a WhatsApp thread — without digging through either.

Home answers "is everything alright." Documents answers "where is the one thing I need right now." Conflating them is exactly how the old Vault felt like a filing cabinet.

Why these calls

Four decisions, and the reasoning behind each.

01

Status before documents, everywhere

"24 documents pending" as someone's first experience of a brand-new property is an accusation, not a welcome — it opens the relationship as debt instead of care. Status chip first; document count is a detail.

02

No jargon, ever, in the primary flow

"Search Report / EC" and "7/12 Extract" are for people who already work in this domain. This person doesn't need to learn a taxonomy of Indian land records to trust that Vault is handling it.

03

WhatsApp is a first-class input, not a fallback

The brief's own scenario has the EC "somewhere in a WhatsApp thread" — for this person, a document is more likely sitting in a chat with their lawyer than saved as a file on a laptop.

04

One small status vocabulary, reused deliberately

"Needs a look" means "still building this property's record" on day one, and "a ₹9,00,000 loan was just registered against it" three months later — same amber banner, same words, very different stakes underneath.

One direction I rejected

"Ask Vault anything" — a chat interface

The obvious move, once AI enters the conversation, is a chatbot. I sketched it and dropped it.

✕

It hands the burden of knowing what to ask back to the user — exactly the opposite of "watches, notices, and tells them."

✕

It fails the two-tap rule outright. Someone shouldn't need a question already in mind before Vault gives them an answer.

Where AI does belong

The sentence that turns "a new entry was filed in the Encumbrance Certificate" into:

"A loan of ₹9,00,000 was registered against this property on 22 August."

Dense legal filings → one plain sentence, delivered without being asked for.

Where AI helped, and where I overrode it

This entire design — concept, screens, and write-up — was built in conversation with Claude, iterating screen by screen rather than handed a brief once and left alone.

✓

Where it helped

  • → Generating the volume of states a real product needs fast — empty, error, multi-item, returning-user.
  • → Acting as a second reviewer: caught that a missing-documents list was still using registrar jargon after I'd already "fixed" the screen once.
✕

Where I overrode it

  • → First visual instinct: an earthy, ledger-and-wax-stamp aesthetic. Dropped once checked against Landeed's actual brand (Electric Violet, DM Sans, the elephant mark).
  • → A copy line promising "Landeed will fetch these documents for you" — flagged as a commitment that only holds if a real backend capability exists behind it.
